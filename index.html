About

Most of my work sits between sensors and software. I like the part where a measurement stops being a number in a terminal and turns into something a person can actually read and act on.

The benchmarks and the web side of Kinevia are what my master's thesis is being built from.

Games are the other half. They are where I get to build a whole system on my own, from the physics to the interface to the models, and where nobody minds if I rewrite it twice to understand it better.

Work

  • Disdrometer sensing for road safety

    My bachelor's thesis. A Thies laser precipitation monitor streams particle data over RS-485, and a Python ETL service validates it, derives the rainfall parameters and stores them. It describes a rain event in far more detail than an ordinary gauge can.

    Read the paper
    bachelor's thesis

  • Web framework benchmarks

    FastAPI against Django, on x86-64 and on ARM, under both HTTP and WebSocket load. Two rounds of measurements, with the methodology and the scripts written to be run again by someone else.

    MDPI article, September

  • Kinevia

    A motion analysis system for clinical use, developed at the university research centre as a colleague's master's thesis. My part is the web side: the NiceGUI interface and the Python backend that records a session, keeps the video in sync with the sensor data and puts the result in front of a doctor.

    my part: web and backend

  • 3D RPG in Unity

    Terrain generated from noise, villages placed on the flat ground it finds, NPCs with branching dialogue. Everything is written rather than dragged in, which is the whole point of it.

    in progress

  • Low-poly assets

    Models I make in Blender for that game. The ones I am happy with go up on itch.io for anyone to use.

    on itch.io

  • Top-down RPG in C++

    SFML and no engine. Turn-based combat on a grid, pathfinding that has to walk around trees, and an interface built from scratch. It taught me more than any tutorial did.

    learning project
